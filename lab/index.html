Swami Pranavananda AI & Robotics Lab

One room at Bharat Sevashram Sangha, Sriniwaspuri, New Delhi. It seats fifteen learners at a time.

The lab was built inside the Sangha's Sriniwaspuri centre and opened in March 2026. It is a working classroom rather than a showroom. Everything in it is there because a class needs it.

What is in the room

  • Workstations 5 desktops
  • Each with 27-inch monitor, webcam, headphones
  • Teaching screen 65-inch interactive smart board
  • Single-board computers Raspberry Pi
  • Electronics Full Arduino sensor kit
  • Smart home corner Voice assistant, smart bulb, smart plug
  • Power backup 6 KVA online UPS
  • Internet Fibre broadband, WiFi throughout
  • Also Laptop and printer

The teaching screen

Every class is taught from this screen so that all fifteen learners can follow the same thing at the same time. When a prompt is typed, everyone watches the answer come back. When a circuit is drawn, everyone sees the same diagram.

Five workstations, and why that number matters

Five workstations is what the room holds, and that shapes how we teach. During a weekend class the batch of fifteen rotates through them in groups of three, so nobody spends the session watching somebody else work.

During weekday practice the slot is capped at five learners, one at each machine, for two hours. That is the part where the learning actually settles.

Hardware you can hold

There are breadboards, LEDs, resistors, jumper wires and an Arduino board in a lab that teaches AI, and people ask why.

Because a machine you have built with your own hands stops being magic. A learner who has watched an LED stay dark because one wire sits in the wrong hole has learned, physically, that the machine only does what it has been given. That lesson carries. It is the same lesson as a wrong GST figure from a chatbot, taught with a wire instead of a sentence.
